Only option (B) was justified, so only (B) was run: a fine-tune of ONLY the SVD temporal VAE decoder on the real right eye of
ALL 291 GT-valid train clips (1164 windows, 16296 frames; latent of the frame -> the frame; LPIPS-VGG + L1; full deployed
576x1024 frames, 2000 steps).  It FAILED its pre-registered dev gate: no checkpoint was eligible and none improved the primary
metric, so -- as pre-registered -- nothing was taken to the 12 test clips.  Test clips were rendered by this lane only for the
passive latent capture (every capture md5 == the published lossless render) and were never scored.
  * DEV (5 registered clips x {origin, deliverable}, same sampled latents, fine-tuned minus stock decoder): REG_FRAME LPIPS-Alex
    +0.0009 / +0.0009 / +0.0016 / +0.0010 / +0.0019 at steps 250 / 500 / 1000 / 1500 / 2000 (better on 4, 4, 2, 2, 2 of 10
    cells); UNREG +0.0003..+0.0012; NIQE worse by +0.35..+0.54 at every step (worse on 12/12 cells at s1000); MUSIQ -0.9..-1.1;
    DISTS +0.0001..+0.0010.  Moving slightly favourably: LPIPS-VGG (the training-loss family, -0.0024..-0.0043) and registered
    PSNR (+0.005..+0.054 dB at 4 of 5 steps, -0.003 at s2000).  [TABLE_DEV_main_v1.txt, SELECTION_DEV_main_v1.json]
  * The decoder did change in a measurable, mostly favourable way on REAL latents, but that does not carry over to the models'
    latents.  On the real right eye of the held-out dev clips (decoder-only round trip, registration-free): LPIPS-Alex
    0.0452 -> 0.0423..0.0430 (-5..-6 %), DISTS 0.0284 -> 0.0269..0.0278 (-2..-5 %), PSNR 35.53 -> 36.42..36.67 dB (part of the
    PSNR gain may be a colour/bias correction: the fine-tuned decoder shifts the mean colour by up to -3.4 levels on R, 0082)
    [ROUNDTRIP_DEV_main_v1.json].  Applied to origin's and the deliverable's sampled latents it ADDS flat-region high-frequency
    energy (flatHF +5..+8 %, stripe energy +3..+6 %, both already above the real right eye's level) and REMOVES edge energy
    (edgeHF -2..-4 %) [DEV_LEVELS_main_v1.txt].  0245 shows the contrast most sharply: the best GT round-trip gain of all dev
    clips (0.0703 -> 0.0631) but the largest output degradation (+0.005..+0.008 REG_FRAME).
  * SCOPE OF "CLOSED": decoder-only fine-tuning on clean GT encoder latents, LPIPS-VGG + L1 (1:1), lr 2e-5, 2000 steps
    (0.49 epoch), evaluated on the two models' sampled latents.  NOT tested: a decoder adapted on the models' OWN output
    latents (the domain gap found here), other loss weightings (e.g. L1-dominant), longer training.  HYPOTHESIS (not shown):
    that the VAE-stage loss blur_diag measured is mostly the 4-channel latent bottleneck rather than decoder capacity -- this
    run recovered only ~6 % of the real-latent round-trip LPIPS, but with 0.49 epoch at lr 2e-5 that is a lower bound, not a
    ceiling.  A post-hoc latent check (section 5, dev only): at latent-cell scale the sampled latents track their own input's
    structure (Laplacian corr 0.77 origin / 0.72 deliverable at the input's edge cells, magnitude 0.94x / 1.01x); this check
    cannot resolve sub-8-px detail (a 3x3 Laplacian on the 72x128 latent responds to ~16-24 px structure), and its
    consecutive-frame reference (0.38) is motion-dependent, not a ceiling.  For fine detail, blur_diag's pixel-space numbers
    remain the evidence that the blur relative to the input forms largely in the VAE path; this check is consistent with
    that.  Flat-region latent energy differs (origin 0.74x, deliverable 1.05x of the input's); why the fine-tuned decoder
    responds differently to sampled latents than to clean encoder latents is not isolated here.  A decoder adapted ON the
    sampled latents is an untested variant, but it inherits a target problem (sampled latents have no pixel-aligned target:
    the warped input carries splat stripes, and the registered real right eye fits only ~half the cells within +-2 px -- the
    same issue that ruled out (A)) and costs ~3 GPU-min per clip per model just to sample train latents (50 clips x 2 models
    ~ 5 GPU-h before training).

5. POST-HOC LATENT CHECK (dev only, after the selection; latent_hf_check_v2.py -> LATENT_HF_CHECK_v2.json; v1 = magnitudes only)
=====================================================================================================================================
Per captured window: INPUT = deployed encode of the model's own warped conditioning frames x 0.18215 (geometry-aligned with
the outputs by construction); per-cell HF = |latent Laplacian| (4 channels); cells exclude any disocclusion pixel (dilated by
one cell); EDGE = top 20 % of non-hole cells by the input frame's gradient, FLAT = bottom 50 %.  Crude latent-space measure;
input-edge cells can include splat stripes.
                       EDGE HF ratio out/in   FLAT HF ratio out/in   Laplacian corr out vs in (EDGE / ALL non-hole)
  origin               0.94 (0.86-1.06)       0.74 (0.58-1.02)       0.77 / 0.68   (per clip EDGE 0.63-0.93)
  deliverable          1.01 (0.92-1.09)       1.05 (0.92-1.14)       0.72 / 0.60   (per clip EDGE 0.56-0.89)
  reference: input latents of consecutive frames t, t+1: corr 0.38 / 0.38 (0.01-0.90, motion-dependent)
Reading (scoped): at latent-cell scale (~16-24 px structure) the sampled latents track their own input's structure at about
the input's magnitude; the check cannot resolve sub-8-px detail.  It is consistent with blur_diag's pixel-space finding that
the blur relative to the input forms largely in the VAE path (decoded input keeps 0.733 of the input's edge energy), but it
does not establish it.  The fine-tuned decoder raised flat-region pixel energy on BOTH models' outputs (section 4), including origin, whose
flat-cell latent energy is below its input's (0.74x) -- so the decoder's response to sampled latents differs from its response
to clean encoder latents; the cause of that difference is not isolated here.
