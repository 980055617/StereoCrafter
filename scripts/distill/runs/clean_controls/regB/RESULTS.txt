(a) WHERE DOES THE DAMAGE LIVE -- the regB step200 weights sampled with 25 Euler steps on 0301 (infer/xcheck_hybrid_regB_ll_steps.py,
    MINIFT_STEPS=25, same hook, FFV1; scores/scores_regB_s25.txt):
        regB step200 @ 8 steps   0.5959   sharp 0.0189      (+0.1609 vs deployed origin 0.4351)
        regB step200 @ 25 steps  0.4314   sharp 0.0287      (+0.0328 vs origin+s25 0.3986 / 0.0295;  -0.0037 vs deployed origin, at 3.1x the sampler cost)
    The same weights that cost +0.161 on the deployed 8-step trajectory cost +0.033 on the 25-step one, and the sharpness collapse
    (0.0189) disappears (0.0287 vs s25's 0.0295).  ~80 % of the deployed damage is therefore the interaction between the fine-tuned
    denoiser and the compute-limited 8-step Euler trajectory, not a destroyed denoiser -- the per-sample loss optimises single-step
    denoising accuracy, which is not what the 8-step sampler needs.  (One clip, one checkpoint; a direct reading, not a theorem.)
(b) IS THE APPROXIMATE REGISTRATION THE LIMITER -- per-frame-registered training (train/xcheck_mini_ft_regB_pf.py, run dir

        best by sampled LPIPS = step300 -> held-out 0204: 0.2388 (+0.0335), sharp 0.0066     (global-shift run step300: 0.2403)
        LPIPSreg (vs registered GT): 0.5911 / 0.6013 / 0.5759 on 0301; 0.1760 on 0204
